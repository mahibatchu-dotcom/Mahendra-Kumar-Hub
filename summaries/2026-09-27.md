# A Reel Big Difference
*2026-09-27* · [Original article](https://www.dot.news/post/6ab78582e07d740002ccf3ef)

**In one line:**
The article explains the history of motion pictures and the technical differences between standard films and the IMAX format.

**Key points:**
- IMAX is experiencing a record-breaking box office run, putting 2026 on track to be its biggest year, though the company is reportedly up for sale.
- The concept of motion pictures originated in 1872 when photographer Eadweard Muybridge captured sequential photos of a running horse to settle a bet for Leland Stanford.
- Standard cinema historically relied on 35mm analog film running vertically through a projector at 24 frames per second.
- Standard 70mm film runs vertically with a 5-perforation pull per frame, creating a wide 2.20:1 aspect ratio.
- True IMAX uses 70mm film run horizontally with a 15-perforation pull, resulting in an image three times larger than standard 70mm with a nearly square 1.43:1 aspect ratio.

**Numbers and names to remember:**
- 1872: The year Leland Stanford made a bet about a horse's stride, leading to the invention of motion pictures.
- Leland Stanford: American industrialist and founder of Stanford University.
- Eadweard Muybridge: The photographer who captured the first sequential rapid-motion images.
- 35mm and 70mm: Physical widths of standard movie film strips.
- 24 frames per second (fps): The speed at which film frames are projected to show smooth movement.
- 15 perforations: The number of sprocket holes pulled per frame in horizontal IMAX projection.
- 1.43:1 and 2.20:1: The aspect ratios of IMAX and standard 70mm film, respectively.
- 2026: The year on track to be IMAX's biggest year yet.

**Why it matters:**
Understanding the technical mechanics behind different film formats explains why "true" IMAX delivers unmatched image detail and a uniquely shaped screen experience compared to standard theaters. 

**Key terms:**
- Analog film: A transparent plastic strip coated with light-sensitive chemicals used to record images.
- Perforations (perfs): Small sprocket holes along the edges of a film strip used to pull it through a projector.
- Frame: An individual photo on a strip of film.
- Aspect ratio: The relationship between the width and the height of an image.
