# Closing The Loop: Delhi is set to witness protests. Again.
*2026-10-10* · [Original article](https://www.dot.news/post/6ac90ed7a2f59e0002cc3f6d)

**In one line:** Opposition groups and the Cockroach Janta Party are protesting in Delhi over election administration controversies, facing strict security lockdowns and police restrictions.

**Key points:**
- Protesters are demanding the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar, the cancellation of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) voter list cleanup, and the repeal of the 2023 Election Commission selection law.
- The Congress party has called for returning to paper ballots, while opposition leaders accuse authorities of voter list fraud and mass disenfranchisement.
- The Supreme Court clarified that it did not approve disputed extra questions added to Form 6 voter application forms during the SIR.
- Delhi Police denied protest permission at Jantar Mantar due to short notice and safety concerns, enforcing Section 163 BNSS restrictions.
- Authorities deployed 23,000 central armed police, set up 10 detention centers, cancelled 45 trains, and requested the closure of 57 metro stations.
- Supreme Court judges observed that while police can regulate movements, regulatory measures should not bring public transport and daily life to a complete halt.

**Numbers and names to remember:**
- **People:** Gyanesh Kumar (Chief Election Commissioner), Mallikarjun Kharge (Congress President), Rahul Gandhi (Leader of the Opposition in Lok Sabha), Priyanka Vadra, Surya Kant (Chief Justice of India), Joymalya Bagchi (Supreme Court Judge), Dharmendra Pradhan, Sonam Wangchuk.
- **Organisations:** Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), Congress, Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), Election Commission (EC), Supreme Court, Delhi Police, Ministry of Home Affairs, Central Armed Police Forces (CAPF).
- **Places:** Delhi, Jantar Mantar, Central Delhi.
- **Numbers/Dates:** 2023 (EC law year), January 2025 (voter list), 10,000 (alleged detainees), 23,000 (CAPF personnel), 10 (detention centres), 45 (cancelled trains), 57 (shut metro stations).

**Why it matters:**
The situation underscores deep political conflict over the independence and fairness of India's electoral process. It also highlights the tension between citizens' constitutional right to protest and state measures used to maintain public order.

**Key terms:**
- **Special Intensive Revision (SIR):** An official process used to clean up and update voter registration lists.
- **Form 6:** The application form used by first-time voters or people changing their voting constituency.
- **Section 163 of BNSS:** A legal provision that allows authorities to issue prohibitory orders against public gatherings in specific areas.
