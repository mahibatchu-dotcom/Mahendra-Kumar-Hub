# Closing The Loop: Jantar Mantar and Connaught Place
*2026-10-17* · [Original article](https://www.dot.news/post/6ad24fc9bd32350002845d7a)

**In one line:** The Delhi Police has de-designated Jantar Mantar as an official protest site following Supreme Court observations and restricted marches in Connaught Place, sparking debate over protest rights and public order.

**Key points:**
- The Delhi Police announced that Jantar Mantar is no longer a designated protest site, citing Supreme Court concerns over basic amenities, traffic management, and nearby sensitive government infrastructure.
- Three alternative demonstration sites were named: Ramlila Ground in Central Delhi, Burari Ground in North Delhi, and CBD Ground in East Delhi.
- Protesters from the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) opposed the move, calling it an arbitrary decision that harms the fundamental right to protest.
- Booking Ramlila Ground costs ₹50,000 per day and has virtually no open dates for the next two and a half months.
- Police blocked CJP's planned "Lehra Do Tiranga" march in Connaught Place using Section 163 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS).
- Traders supported restricting protests to protect local businesses during the festive season, while Union Home Minister Amit Shah criticized the demonstrations as manufactured events.

**Numbers and names to remember:**
- **People:** Saurav Das (co-convenor of CJP), Dharmendra Pradhan, Sonam Wangchuk, Anna Hazare, Amit Shah (Union Home Minister).
- **Places:** Jantar Mantar, Connaught Place, Ramlila Ground, Burari Ground, CBD Ground, Delhi.
- **Organisations:** Delhi Police, Supreme Court, Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), National Green Tribunal (NGT), Municipal Corporation of Delhi, Ministry of Home Affairs, BJP, Congress.
- **Numbers and Laws:** ₹50,000 per day booking fee, 2.5 months of unavailable booking dates, 8,000 people, 1.5-km area, 14 parties, Section 163 of BNSS.

**Why it matters:**
The removal of Jantar Mantar as a protest hub shifts public demonstrations away from the seat of political power in New Delhi. It underlines the ongoing legal and social tension between citizens' right to assembly and urban administrative concerns regarding security, traffic, and business livelihoods.

**Key terms:**
- **Section 163 of BNSS:** A law permitting government authorities to issue orders banning gatherings in specified public places.
- **Designated protest site:** An officially approved venue where public demonstrations and assemblies are allowed by authorities.
