# Trust Issues On The Internet: What went wrong with eMudhra’s digital certificates?
*2026-10-26* · [Original article](https://www.dot.news/post/6adcac879208d10002517099)

**In one line:** Bengaluru-based cybersecurity firm eMudhra lost over 40% of its stock value after a critical flaw was discovered in eight of its intermediate digital certificates.

**Key points:**
- Cybersecurity company eMudhra's stock price dropped by more than 40% starting October 21.
- The stock crash wiped out over ₹1,300 crore in market value for the company.
- The drop was triggered by the discovery of a critical flaw in 8 of eMudhra's intermediate digital certificates.
- eMudhra acts as a globally trusted Certificate Authority, which helps secure internet browsing through HTTPS encryption.
- HTTPS relies on trusted third-party certificates to prevent data eavesdropping, tampering, and fake website scams.

**Numbers and names to remember:**
- eMudhra: Bengaluru-based cybersecurity company listed on the stock exchange since 2022.
- October 21: The date when eMudhra's stock price began to fall sharply.
- Over 40%: The drop in eMudhra's stock price within a few hours.
- Over ₹1,300 crore: The amount of market value lost by eMudhra.
- 8: The number of intermediate digital certificates found to have a critical flaw.

**Why it matters:**
Flaws in digital certificates weaken the foundation of internet security, making web users vulnerable to identity spoofing and intercepted data. For the company involved, such security failures can lead to severe loss of financial value and trust.

**Key terms:**
- Certificate Authority (CA): A trusted third-party organisation that issues digital identity certificates for websites.
- HTTPS: A secure web protocol that encrypts data sent between a user's browser and a website server.
- DNS (Domain Name System): The internet's address book that translates website URLs into numerical IP addresses.
- Man-in-the-Middle attack: A security attack where a hacker intercepts and potentially changes communications between a browser and a website.
- Digital certificate (X.509): An electronic document that verifies a website's identity using cryptographic signatures.
