# Can Apple Take a Bite of UPI?
*2026-10-30* · [Original article](https://www.dot.news/post/6ae338faf25526000255c3da)

**In one line:** Apple Pay has launched in India through Axis Bank, offering a secure card payment alternative in a market dominated by UPI.

**Key points:**
- Apple Pay launched in India with Axis Bank as its first partner, but it currently does not support UPI, RuPay, debit cards, or credit cards from major banks like SBI, HDFC, and ICICI.
- Traditional card payments incur a Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) of 1% to 3%, which is shared among issuing banks, payment gateways, and network providers like Visa or Mastercard.
- Apple Pay uses Near Field Communication (NFC) technology to allow users to make contactless payments with an iPhone, Apple Watch, or iPad.
- To improve security, Apple Pay uses tokenisation, replacing real card numbers with a Device Account Number (DAN) stored in a hardware chip called the Secure Element.
- Using tokenisation can reduce payment fraud by up to 26%.

**Numbers and names to remember:**
- Tim Cook: Apple CEO who discussed bringing Apple Pay to India.
- Axis Bank: The initial banking partner for Apple Pay in India.
- SBI, HDFC, ICICI: Major Indian banks currently not supported by Apple Pay at launch.
- Visa and Mastercard: Card networks that route card transactions and collect a network assessment fee of around 0.15%.
- NPCI: National Payments Corporation of India.
- 1% to 3%: The typical Merchant Discount Rate charged on card transactions.
- 26%: The potential reduction in payment fraud from using tokenisation.

**Why it matters:**
Apple Pay aims to make card transactions faster and safer in India, but it faces a difficult path to adoption because it lacks support for UPI and major Indian banks.

**Key terms:**
- Merchant Discount Rate (MDR): A fee charged to merchants on card payments, usually ranging between 1% and 3%.
- Near Field Communication (NFC): Short-range wireless technology used for contactless payments.
- Tokenisation: Replacing actual credit or debit card details with a substitute code to prevent fraud.
- Device Account Number (DAN): A unique, substitute card number assigned to a specific device for secure transactions.
- Secure Element: A dedicated chip inside an Apple device that safely stores tokenised card data.
