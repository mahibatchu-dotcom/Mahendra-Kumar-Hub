# The Science Of Staying Up
*2026-11-01* · [Original article](https://www.dot.news/post/6ae6130540409b000234ffb4)

**In one line:** This article explains the actual physics behind airplane flight, covering the four forces of flight, how jet engines produce thrust, and common misconceptions about aerodynamic lift.

**Key points:**
- Albert Einstein attempted to design an airplane wing in 1917, but test pilots found it unstable and Einstein later dismissed it as a youthful mistake.
- Four main forces govern flight: weight pulling down, lift pushing up, thrust pushing forward, and drag pulling backward.
- Jet engines create forward thrust by compressing intake air, igniting fuel, and expelling hot expanding gases out the back.
- Jet engines also generate onboard electricity, backed up by an Auxiliary Power Unit (APU) and an emergency Ram Air Turbine (RAT).
- The common school explanation that air must move faster over a wing's curved top to catch up with air underneath (the "Longer Path" theory) is incorrect.

**Numbers and names to remember:**
- 1905: year Albert Einstein published his Special Theory of Relativity.
- 1916: year Einstein published his paper "Elementary Theory of Water Waves and of Flight".
- 1917: year Einstein designed his cat's-back wing.
- 1954: year Einstein referred to his wing design as a "youthful folly".
- Albert Einstein: theoretical physicist who attempted to design an airplane wing.
- LVG: German aircraft manufacturer that built a plane using Einstein's wing design.

**Why it matters:**
Understanding the true mechanics of flight dispels widespread scientific misconceptions taught in classrooms. It shows how fundamental laws of physics work together to keep heavy modern aircraft safely in the air.

**Key terms:**
- Airfoil: the cross-sectional shape of an aircraft wing, typically curved on top and flatter on the bottom.
- Drag: resistance created when an object moves through a fluid like air.
- Thrust: the forward pushing force created by a jet engine.
- APU (Auxiliary Power Unit): a small engine in the tail of a plane that provides electrical power and starts the main engines.
- RAT (Ram Air Turbine): an emergency generator that deploys into the airstream to power vital systems if main power fails.
