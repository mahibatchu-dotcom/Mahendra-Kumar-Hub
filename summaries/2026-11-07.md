# Closing The Loop: We talk about the US midterm elections
*2026-11-07* · [Original article](https://www.dot.news/post/6aeb0f7d1a11830002b00775)

**In one line:** An overview of how the U.S. political system and midterm elections work, alongside the key differences between the Democratic and Republican parties.

**Key points:**
- The U.S. President serves a four-year term, while Congress consists of a 100-member Senate (six-year terms) and a 435-member House of Representatives (two-year terms).
- Midterm elections occur at the exact midpoint of a president's term, putting all 435 House seats and about one-third of Senate seats up for vote.
- Controlling Congress is vital because both chambers must approve bills before they can become federal law.
- The Democratic Party is left-wing, supporting social liberalism, government economic intervention, climate policies, and higher taxes on the wealthy.
- The Republican Party (GOP) is right-wing, focusing on traditional values, lower taxes, reduced government spending, and strict immigration controls.
- Republicans currently hold thin majorities under President Donald Trump, with 218 seats in the House and 53 seats in the Senate.

**Numbers and names to remember:**
- **435:** Total seats in the U.S. House of Representatives.
- **100:** Total seats in the U.S. Senate.
- **218:** House seats held by Republicans (the minimum needed for a majority).
- **53:** Senate seats held by Republicans.
- **Donald Trump:** Current U.S. President.
- **Democratic Party and Republican Party (GOP):** The two main political parties in the U.S.

**Why it matters:**
Midterm elections determine which party controls Congress, directly shaping how much power a sitting president has to pass federal laws and execute their agenda.

**Key terms:**
- **POTUS:** President of the United States.
- **Midterm elections:** U.S. Congressional and state elections held halfway through a president's four-year term.
- **Electoral College:** A group of electors pledged to presidential candidates who officially vote to elect the president.
- **GOP:** "Grand Old Party," the nickname for the Republican Party.
