# Turning Red Ink Black
*2026-11-22* · [Original article](https://www.dot.news/post/693296485f68840002ea9e23)

**In one line:** This article traces the history of "Black Friday," explaining how a term once tied to financial crashes and traffic chaos became a major shopping event.

**Key points:**
- The first "Black Friday" occurred on September 24, 1869, when a U.S. gold market crash dropped prices from $160 to $130 per ounce.
- In the 1950s, Philadelphia police used "Black Friday" to describe heavy traffic and crowd chaos caused by shoppers and Army–Navy football fans after Thanksgiving.
- A 1950s trade magazine noted severe worker absenteeism the day after Thanksgiving, calling it "Friday-after-Thanksgiving-itis."
- Businesses eventually turned the Friday after Thanksgiving into a paid holiday to keep factory production on track.
- Retailers initially tried rebranding the day as "Big Friday" to remove negative connotations before using marketing to reframe the term.

**Numbers and names to remember:**
- Jay Gould and James Fisk: Wall Street investors who tried to manipulate the gold market in 1869.
- Sept. 24, 1869: The original "Black Friday," when gold dropped from $160 to $130 per ounce after the U.S. government sold $4 million in gold.
- Philadelphia: The city where police used the term in the 1950s.
- Grace McFeeley: A Philadelphia merchant representative mentioned in 1981.

**Why it matters:**
The story reveals how smart marketing successfully transformed a negative term associated with economic crashes, severe traffic, and missing workers into a famous global shopping festival.

**Key terms:**
- Gold bullion: Gold in the form of bars or bulk quantity.
- Absenteeism: Employees calling in sick or missing work.
- Thanksgiving: A U.S. holiday originating from a 1600s harvest feast between colonists and Native Americans.
